Prediction of Academic Performance using Social Cognitive Determiners

Prineeth Ramachandra

Received 15 November 2026.

This paper reports a small classroom experiment based on Social Cognitive Theory. Three 8th grade learners at a municipal school in Ahmedabad completed a self-efficacy survey, then sat a PISA test on three separate days, each with a different morning at home and a different place to take the test. Their scores are given alongside observations from each day.

social cognitive theory, self-efficacy, classroom environment, assessment

Introduction to Social Cognitive Theory

Perceived self-efficacy is concerned with people’s beliefs in their capabilities to produce given attainments. A person cannot be all things, which would require mastery of every realm of human life. People differ in the areas in which they cultivate their efficacy and in the levels to which they develop it even within their given development paths and growths. The idea of the Social Cognitive Theory is that learning occurs in a social context with a dynamic and reciprocal interaction of the person, environment, and behavior. The unique feature of SCT is the emphasis on social influence and its emphasis on external and internal social reinforcement. These past experiences influences reinforcements, expectations, and expectancies, all of which shape whether a person will engage in a specific behavior and the reasons why a person engages in that behavior. The three subsystems are:

  • Personal characteristics
  • Behavior
  • Environmental conditions

Hypothesis and Initial Thoughts

The deeper we dive into the deliberation and discussion of learning methods and determiners, the more variables we find affecting the final outcome of the decided assessment. Based on my teaching and educational research experience in behavioural science, and pedagogy so far, objectively determining learner behaviour has been quite an uphill challenge, and has not taken consciously taken into consideration many of these factors. Therefore, for us to design programs, structures and actions that can lead to efficient and effective, it is vital for us to collect data, and understand the trends and patterns in every classroom.

I strongly believe that factors like, interior design and layout of the immediate environment that learners spend their time in, overall operating mindsets, stakeholder value systems, quantity and quality of intrinsic motivation, and self-efficacy, are some specific factors that can be controlled for the learners, to then predict their academic performance and growth. It is difficult to see results in qualities like shifts in mindset, values, and skill transfer, as these may take a longer time to impact a learner. However, I think that there will be a huge shift in academic performance based on changes in their immediate environment, interaction with stakeholders, and changes in personal characteristics. The correlation is stronger for academic achievement with this.

Data Collection

We will be using two main sets of assessments to acquire both qualitative and quantitative data for different learners.

The General Self-Efficacy Survey

The General Self-Efficacy Survey (GSES) for learners, is an assessment designed to understand 9 elements of self-efficacy, which are:

  • Self-Efficacy in Enlisting Social Resources
  • Self-Efficacy for Academic Achievement
  • Self-Efficacy for Self-Regulated Learning
  • Self-Efficacy for Leisure Time and Extracurricular Activities
  • Self-Regulatory Efficacy
  • Self-Efficacy to Meet Others’ Expectations
  • Social Self-Efficacy
  • Self-Assertive Efficacy
  • Self-Efficacy for Enlisting Parental and Community Support

(link to survey)

Each element has statements, and the learner with the teacher’s assistance to understand, and to avoid misinterpretation of the question, are expected to answer these questions on a confidence scale of 0-100. To make analysis more comprehensive, this survey was conducted in Channasandra Public School, a Government school in Bengaluru, and in Thakkarbapanagar English School No. 1, an Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation (AMC) school in Ahmedabad. Given the short time frame of the experiment, both schools selected 3 highly reflective learners from 8th grade classrooms. The main reasons for implementing the GSES was in view of the fact that it covers all vital areas where a learner shows positive and negative growth in self-efficacy through particular actions, and it serves as a remarkable tool to provide learners with an opportunity to reflect the state of their current mindsets and operating principles by stating specific examples with their teacher, which can double-up as a great starting point for positive reformative action.

The Programme for International learner Assessment Test

The Programme for International learner Assessment (PISA) test was chosen as a benchmark test to obtain data with respect to direct academic achievement. There were very specific data points that I wanted to get from the PISA test during the administering of the test, they were:

  • Critical Thinking
  • Reading Comprehension
  • Mathematical Thinking
  • Patience and Grit
  • Creativity
  • Problem-solving

Since SCT has three broad subsystems, it made sense for me to design experiences in relation to these subsystems. And thus, I administered the test for three learners from Thakkarbapanagar English School No. 1, over three separate days, strictly controlling their experiences over all three days, from the start of their day, till the point that they started their test. The three learners who participated were Learner A, Learner B and Learner C, all extremely high rigor learners.The learners’ names have been replaced. I was in constant communication with the parents of the above mentioned students, just to keep track, and continuously documenting and controlling their experiences.

Rules for the assessments were communicated with the students and were as follows,

  • No questions and/or doubts will be entertained during the assessment
  • You can talk to yourself, but not to anyone else

To be noted: The assessment was slightly tweaked to better suit the reading comprehension levels of all three students, as they are all on similar levels of mathematical comprehension. This was done as an attempt to eliminate or reduce the ambiguity or uncertainty in result, which could be caused due to the lack of understanding of the question.

The days proceeded as follows:

Day 1: Riverfront

The night before day 1, I informed the parents of all three learners to be extremely kind, and nice to the learners the next morning, and not to point at any problems or make them do anything that they dislike, at the start of the day. I received extremely positive responses from all parents, confirming that the learners are happy, well-fed, and are looking forward to school as they departed from their respective homes. My behavior with the students was similar to that of the parents, where I did not nitpick, or punish any of the kids, and started off class normally. A correlation between this activity and their overall behavior was drawn, as the kids were immensely invested in class that day, and had great focus on topics of discussion. With consent from the parents, the students were taken to a moderately quiet location on Riverfront and made to sit close together in a triangle, and thus began their test.

Day 2: The Office

The night before day 2, the parents were informed to radically change their behavior the next day, and burden them with some work that the learners wouldn’t usually do in the morning. The parents were also informed to behave rudely, and to treat the students a little differently than usual, let alone how they behaved on day 1. Also, the students were not provided with breakfast by the parents. Communications with the parents showed something a little unexpected: two students, Learner B and Learner A were visibly unhappy when they left their homes, however, Learner C was not phased by the seemingly unusual behavior shown by his mother. I continued my usual behavior with students which is quite joyous, and that of enjoyment. Next, the students were taken to Teach For India’s office, to administer their next round of tests. They were seated in a large room, with a couple of other adults also present in the room. This was fairly straightforward, and the room seemed conducive to produce good results in the test. Towards the end of the day, all three students were happy and enjoyed the rigor and relevance of the test that was given. However, something that has to be brought to light was that Learner A and Learner B continued to be unhappy with proceedings from this morning, and this resulted in lesser and weaker interactions with their peers and teachers in school.

Day 3: The Tea Shop

The night before day 3, I informed the parents to behave how they would on a normal day of work, and to treat the learners in a similar manner: normally. Parents informed me that the learners seemed fine, with no real concerns or shifts in behaviors. Although the parents did a stellar job with the experiment, today, Learner A seemed very affected by something. Upon further investigation, it was understood that he had seen something that disturbed him, while he was cycling to school. I did not probe into the issue, simply because I wanted his performance to be determined by all of these individual factors. After school, I had spoken to the owner of a tea shop, and asked if I could conduct the assessment there. It was extremely noisy, and the conditions with respect to infrastructure and environment was not very favourable for the learners. They chose to sit on a small, tilted wooden bench with no table, and began the assessment. The shop was situated in a small gully, with an unbelievable amount of traffic, which resulted in a large amount of air and noise pollution.

Similar instructions and conditions have been given to the teacher at Channasandra Public School, Bengaluru. I am still waiting for the results from this set of assessments for further analysis, and comparisons between the school.

Results

The results from the test was quite surprising, given my experiences with the learners. All three learners understood the implications and impact of self-efficacy in their own personal lives, and not just academically. They found that reflection using this survey was extremely helpful and assisted them in understanding their next steps towards corrective behaviour. They also stated specific values they wanted help with developing, to aid them in tackling problems in the future.

Conclusion

Doing the experiment just reaffirmed the thought that there’s more than meets the eye, with academic performance and setting targets for the same. All three of the students expected themselves to achieve a minimum score of 9/10, but nobody did. The main goal of understanding the patterns is to help effectively improve pedagogy, formulating techniques of classroom management, and possibly working towards the overall improvement of the present curriculum, and I think that this was of huge help towards working a positive step towards that direction.
